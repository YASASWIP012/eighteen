## One line

Child creators are owed money by law. Eighteen lets a family prove it was set aside, without showing anyone the child's income.

## Description

Minnesota, Illinois and California require money to be set aside for minors who earn through online content. A child can have grounds to sue over it, but the record usually sits with the parent, the person they would have to sue.

Eighteen runs each statute as a zero-knowledge circuit, and the deposit has to carry the fiduciary's signature. A deposit one cent short of what the law requires can't produce a proof, and neither can a figure the fiduciary didn't sign. A Chainlink CRE workflow seals what the channel publicly said and commits to the period's earnings, without nodes seeing the figure. A sponsor's USDC only releases when that report, a closed human review and a valid proof all agree. If a title names a child on a video declared as zero participation, the report carries a contradiction and the payout stops on-chain.

The same facts give different answers by state: $20,000 at 50% content owes $5,000 in Minnesota and $6,500 in California. The money stays with a bank or fiduciary, as the statutes require.

Eighteen does not prove that a parent told the truth. It proves who said what and when, and where that conflicts with public facts.
